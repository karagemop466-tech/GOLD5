 16 CFR § 23.18 — Misrepresentation of weight and “total weight.”
official source ↗ · 2026-10-23 · re-read by 6d10ac0a · saved excerpt ↗
eCFR page state: 'This content is from the eCFR and is authoritative but unofficial. Displaying title 16, up to date as of 10/21/2026. Title 16 was last amended 10/19/2026.' Authority: 15 U.S.C. 45, 46. Source: 83 FR 40667, Aug. 16, 2018. Text: '(a) It is unfair or deceptive to misrepresent the weight of a diamond. (b) It is unfair or deceptive to use the word “point” or any abbreviation in any representation, advertising, marking, or labeling to describe the weight of a diamond, unless the weight is also stated as decimal parts of a carat (e.g., 25 points or .25 carat). A carat is a standard unit of weight for a diamond and is equivalent to 200 milligrams (1/5 gram). A point is one one-hundredth (1/100) of a carat.'
Finding: § 23.18 governs DIAMOND weight representations. It does not require disclosure of a ring's gold-alloy gram weight, so the checklist no longer cites it as the basis for the grams criterion; it is cited for what it actually says, and the grams criterion is labelled as this project's buying rule.
