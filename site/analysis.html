What the ratios mean

Under 1.00×
Stop and verify
The price is below the calculated gold value. Confirm weight, metal and authenticity independently before sending money; the ratio alone does not prove intent.
1.0–2.0×
Mostly metal
Plausible for a plain band sold near melt. Still verify solid gold, hallmark and weight — a low ratio can also mean a hollow or plated item.
2.0–8.0×
Normal for a setting
Design and labour dominate. For a diamond ring this is expected; it is not evidence of overcharging.
Over 8×
Stones/brand dominate
Typical of diamond solitaires. Judge the stone separately (certificate, clarity, colour) — metal value tells you nothing about it.

Findings

  • Weight transparency is the bottleneck. Only 7 of 25 listings (28%) publish an exact weight. Without it, no gold-value calculation is possible — so the blank is the finding, not a gap filled by inference.
  • Maker’s marks remain mostly undocumented. 1 of 25 rows states one (a seller claim on the sold listing); 24 do not. Ask for photos and independent verification.
  • “Solid gold” is stated on 9 of 25. The major retailers describe metal and karat but not solidity. That is a question to ask, not an assumption to make.
  • Median ratios are descriptive, not appraisals. All priced weighted rows: 3.20×; complete-ring subset: 6.38×. Diamonds and labour can legitimately dominate price.
  • Below-metal screen: 0. No current exact-weight complete-ring row is below metal; the old case-study conclusion was withdrawn.
  • Maximum observed ratio is 17.52× (Brilliant Round Solitaire 0.71 ctw). Metal value excludes stones, labour and brand; judge those separately.
  • Return terms are a material gap. 21 of 25 rows have a policy that was actually read. Avianne, Jewelry Master, Solid Gold Jewelers and the sold case study remain limited or inapplicable; Etsy windows and exclusions vary.
