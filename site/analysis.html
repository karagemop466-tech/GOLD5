The one genuine red flag7 g Band — case study — 7 g of 14K contains 4.08 g of fine gold, worth $528.64 at spot, yet it was listed at $444.80 (0.84×). That cannot be a sustainable legitimate sale; it is the pattern of a scam listing (or a misdescribed item). It is a sold case study, kept as an example. FL-01

What the ratios mean

Under 1.00×
Stop
The price is below the gold value. Either the item is not what it claims, the weight is wrong, or it is a scam. Do not send money.
1.0–2.0×
Mostly metal
Plausible for a plain band sold near melt. Still verify solid gold, hallmark and weight — a low ratio can also mean a hollow or plated item.
2.0–8.0×
Normal for a setting
Design and labour dominate. For a diamond ring this is expected; it is not evidence of overcharging.
Over 8×
Stones/brand dominate
Typical of diamond solitaires. Judge the stone separately (certificate, clarity, colour) — metal value tells you nothing about it.

Findings

  • Weight transparency is the bottleneck. Only 8 of 25 listings (32%) publish grams. Without a weight, no gold-value calculation is possible — so the blank is the finding, not a gap in research.
  • No listing states a maker’s mark. Two claim a karat stamp; zero provide a photo or a manufacturer’s mark. That fails the FTC’s own test (“if you don’t see a trademark along with the karat mark, don’t buy it”).
  • “Solid gold” is stated on 9 of 25. The major retailers describe metal and karat but not solidity. That is a question to ask, not an assumption to make.
  • The median complete ring asks 4.9× its metal value. That is normal once diamonds and labour are included; the useful screen is the low outlier, not the high one.
  • One listing priced below metal (0.84× if below else "n/a") — the case study that shows why the screen exists.
  • Maximum observed ratio is 17.4× (Brilliant Round Solitaire 0.71 ctw), a diamond solitaire. Metal value is the wrong yardstick for it; the certificate is the right one.
  • Return terms are the second-largest gap. 14 of 25 rows have a policy that was actually read. Brilliant Earth’s policy page redirects; several sellers state only “returns accepted”.
