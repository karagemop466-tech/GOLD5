The no-hallucination rule

No price, weight, karat, hallmark, policy or contact detail appears on this site unless it was read from an official page and quoted verbatim. Values are never inferred, rounded from memory, or carried over from a search snippet. If a seller does not publish a value, the cell stays blank and the row is flagged as unverified.

  • One source per claim. Each table cell traces to a URL and a quote you can open yourself.
  • Arithmetic is labelled. The only computed number is fine-gold value at spot: grams × karat/24 × spot per gram, with the spot observation and timestamp printed.
  • Blank beats wrong. 10 of 12 rows have no gram weight because no listing published one. That is a finding, not a gap to fill in.
  • Re-checked at review time. Every review pass re-runs the hallucination checks described on the Method page.

What the numbers actually say

Four findings that follow directly from the captured data. Each one is arithmetic on verified figures, not an opinion about a brand.

1. The 18K premium is visible and small

Brilliant Earth sells the same Fleur solitaire in 14K and 18K yellow gold. 14K is $1,350 and 18K is $1,550: a difference of $200, or 14.8% more for 16.7 percentage points more fine gold. The metal step-up is cheap relative to the setting, but neither listing publishes a weight, so the premium cannot be checked against grams.

2. Where a weight exists, metal is a small share of the price

These are the only two rows in the shortlist with a seller-published gram weight, so they are the only place the checklist's price-versus-gold test can actually run:

Melt = grams × karat ÷ 24 × $129.51/g. “Price minus metal” is design, labour, margin, duty and packaging — a residual, not a cost breakdown. Multipliers of roughly 4× are normal for light retail gold; a multiplier below 1 would mean the ring is priced under its own metal value, which is the red flag the checklist describes.

3. Settings and complete rings are different products, price-wise

Setting-only prices run from $925 to $2,320, while the single complete ring with a verified price is $2,900 and the plain bands sit at $399–$499. A setting price excludes the centre stone, which is usually the largest line item, so the two ranges cannot be compared directly (flag F-015).

4. The checklist's most important test is the one the market makes hardest

Ten of twelve listings never published a gram weight, and none published a hallmark or maker's mark. The two figures that decide whether a price is fair are therefore unavailable online for almost every candidate — which is exactly why the plan ends with a written request to the seller and an XRF test after delivery.

Most important findings

  • F-004 Only 2 of 12 rows publish a gram weight — The checklist asks for the ring's weight in grams. Ross-Simons publishes a 'Gold Weight' line on plain gold rings (1.3 g and 1.7 g on the two rows that have it), which proves the figure exists and can be requested. The other ten rows — every diamond setting, both Mejuri pieces and the Costco ring — published no gram weight anywhere on the captured page.
  • F-005 No hallmark or maker's mark stated online — No fetched listing stated a hallmark, quality stamp, or maker's mark. The checklist requires a 10K/14K/18K stamp plus a maker's/manufacturer's mark, which can only be confirmed on the physical ring or from a close-up photo.
  • F-006 'Solid gold' wording is missing from most listings — Blue Nile, Brilliant Earth and Ross-Simons product pages state the karat (e.g. '14K Yellow Gold') but do not use the words 'solid gold'. Only the two Mejuri entries explicitly say 'solid gold'. Karat alone does not prove the item is not plated, filled, or vermeil.
  • F-002 Cached search price differed from the live price — A search-index snapshot for the Knife Edge Solitaire showed $700, while the live product page showed '$925(Setting Price)' on 2026-10-22. Prices in this project come only from the live page capture.
  • F-007 Mejuri listing contradicts itself on gold colour — On the Bold Diamond Eternity Ring page the variant selector says 'Solid White Gold (14k), Natural Diamond', while the Materials panel on the same page says 'Solid Yellow Gold (14k)'. Metal colour is therefore not certain from the page.
  • F-009 Costco lists precious metals as non-refundable — Costco's official return policy states a 100% satisfaction guarantee, then lists exceptions. One of them reads 'Precious metals are non-refundable.' The same document carves out diamonds of 1.00 ct or larger, which are returned against a Jewelry Credit Memo after a 48-hour gemologist inspection. Its jewelry FAQ also says jewelry bought on Costco.com can be returned for a refund including shipping. The captured gold ring is a 1.00 ct diamond ring, so which rule governs it is genuinely unclear from the published text.

What this means for buying

Safe path

  1. Pick a row whose seller has verified contact details and a written return window.
  2. Message the seller for the gram weight, the hallmark + maker's mark, and written confirmation the ring is solid 14K/18K gold (not plated, vermeil, filled or overlay).
  3. Run the numbers in the calculator against the captured spot price.
  4. Pay by credit card; keep the listing, the messages and the receipt.
  5. Before wearing or resizing, take it to an independent jeweler for a weight check and XRF purity test.

Walk away if…

  • The seller will not state the gram weight in writing.
  • The listing says “gold” but not the karat, or the price sits far below the fine-gold value at spot.
  • Returns are excluded for resized or custom work and you have not confirmed the size.
  • Payment must be irreversible (wire, crypto, gift card, peer-to-peer app).
  • No one will let you have the ring independently tested.
