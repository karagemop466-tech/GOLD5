Enter the weight the seller gives you in writing. A multiplier below about 1 means the price is at or under the metal value — treat that as a red flag, because labour and setting cost money. Multipliers of roughly 2–4 are typical for retail settings; this is a sanity check, not an appraisal.

This is not the ring's weight. It divides the asking price by the spot value of one gram of pure gold, which answers a different question: if you spent this money on raw metal instead, how much fine gold would it buy? A setting price that buys only a gram or two of metal is normal, because labour and design dominate. Once a seller publishes the actual gram weight, the same division in reverse becomes a real “is this unusually cheap?” test.

Why this shortlist cannot yet answer “is it unusually cheap?”

The checklist says to compare the price with the ring's gold weight. That needs a gram figure, and 10 of the 12 listings published none in the page text captured here (flag F-004).
