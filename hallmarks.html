What the marks must show

Near the karat quality mark, look for the name or the U.S. registered trademark of the company that stands behind the mark. The trademark may be a name, a symbol, or initials. The FTC warns: if you are thinking about buying a piece of gold jewelry but don't see a trademark along with the karat mark, don't buy it.

Requirement What to look for Official source
Karat quality mark Tells how much pure gold the piece contains (e.g., 10K, 14K, 18K) FTC — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry
Maker's / manufacturer's mark Name, U.S. registered trademark, symbol, or initials standing behind the mark FTC — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry
Legal marking duty Anyone who applies a quality mark indicating gold/silver content must also apply their trademark or name to the article (National Stamping Act, 15 U.S.C. 297) eCFR — 16 CFR § 23.10
No deception on fineness Misrepresenting the quantity or karat fineness of gold is unfair or deceptive eCFR — 16 CFR § 23.3

Terms that are NOT solid gold

Marking / term FTC description Solid gold? Source
Gold filled / gold overlay / rolled gold plate (RGP) A layer of at least 10 karat gold mechanically applied to a base metal; must be marked with karat quality and the plating term (e.g., "14K gold overlay"). If the gold layer is less than 1/20 of the item's weight, the fraction must be stated (e.g., "1/40 14K gold overlay"). No FTC
Gold electroplate At least 10 karat gold, at least 0.175 microns thick, applied by an electrolytic process on a base metal. No FTC
Gold flashed / gold washed Very thin electroplating; wears away faster than gold plate, gold filled, or gold electroplate. No FTC
Vermeil Sterling silver base coated or plated with gold. No FTC
"Solid gold" on a hollow item Items with a hollow center must not be marked or described as "solid gold" (must disclose, e.g., "14K Gold Tubing"). No (misleading if marked solid) FTC · 16 CFR § 23.3
Important limitation: In the U.S., hallmarking is not government pre-sale assay verification. The FTC requires the karat mark to be accompanied by the sponsor's trademark, but a karat stamp alone is not proof of purity. That is why independent testing after delivery (see Independent Verification) is the key safeguard. This limitation is listed on the Flagged for Review page.
