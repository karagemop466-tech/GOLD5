Method

How every line was verified

This site is the output of a repeatable process, not a set of opinions. Here is exactly what “verified” means in this project, what it does not mean, and how to re-run the checks yourself.

The no-hallucination rule, restated

No price, weight, karat, policy, or seller detail is published here unless it is backed by a verbatim quote from an official page and that page's URL. If a value could not be confirmed, it is left blank and flagged as unverified. Prices were captured on the date shown and can change.

Protocol

  1. Capture, don't recall. Each product page is fetched on the verification date; the price, metal, width, stock number and stone data are copied as display strings, together with the surrounding text as a quote.
  2. One quote per field. A value is stored only if that value is a literal substring of the quoted evidence. The verifier enforces this mechanically — a remembered price cannot pass.
  3. Policies are quoted, not summarised. Return windows and exclusions come from the retailer's own policy page, including the sentence about resized and personalised items.
  4. Contact details are text on the seller's site. Phone numbers and emails are copied from the page; if a page does not show one, the field is empty and a flag is raised (F-011).
  5. Nothing is inferred. No weight, hallmark or “solid gold” claim is derived from karat, brand name or price.
  6. Arithmetic is explicit. Derived values print their formula and the exact spot observation they used.
  7. Blanks are findings. A missing value becomes a row in the Flagged register with the action needed.

What “verified” does and does not mean

It does mean

  • The value was published on that official page on 22 October 2026 (UTC).
  • The exact sentence is stored and shown on the row.
  • You can re-open the link and check it yourself.

It does not mean

  • That the ring is authentic — that needs a physical test (XRF) by an independent jeweler.
  • That the price is current — re-check it on the live page.
  • That the seller's marketing claim about purity is true.
  • That a karat stamp proves the alloy; a stamp can be counterfeit.

Scope and limits of this pass

  • 12 entries from five retailers were captured. This is a research shortlist, not the market.
  • 17 flagged items. The structural gap is the missing hallmark or maker's mark (F-005) on every row; gram weights are published for only two rows (F-004).
  • Search snippets and cached prices were rejected as evidence wherever a live page could be read instead (F-002, F-003, F-013, F-016). Where a snippet is the only captured text, the capture method says so on the row.
  • All pages are static HTML generated from data/*.json. Rebuilding without changing the data must produce byte-identical files, which the CI checks.

Reproduce it

python3 scripts/build_site.py      # regenerate the site from data/
python3 scripts/build_site.py --check   # fail if the committed HTML is stale
python3 scripts/verify.py         # quote, arithmetic, coverage and link passes
python3 scripts/verify.py --links # also re-fetch every URL and report HTTP status
