Hallmark — Karat Stamp + Maker's Mark

Look for a 10K/14K/18K stamp plus a maker's/manufacturer's mark. A karat stamp by itself does NOT prove the ring is actually that purity.

Mark Type What It Means Required? Source Verified Source Link
Karat quality mark Tells how much pure gold the piece contains (e.g., 14K, 18K, 585, 750) Yes Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Maker's / manufacturer's mark Name or U.S. registered trademark of the company that stands behind the karat mark Yes Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Karat stamp alone NOT sufficient proof of purity — must be accompanied by maker's mark No Verified FTC Consumer Guide

What to Check

  • Look inside the ring band for a karat mark (10K, 14K, 18K, etc.)
  • Look for a maker's mark — name, symbol, or initials near the karat mark
  • If there is a karat mark but NO maker's mark, the FTC advises: don't buy it
  • Common fineness stamps: 585 = 14K, 750 = 18K, 417 = 10K, 999 = 24K

Source: FTC — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry

⚠️ Important Warning

A 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is actually that purity. The safest approach is: reputable seller + proper markings + reasonable price + independent testing after delivery.
