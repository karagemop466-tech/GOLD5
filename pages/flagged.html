F-009 Critical Costco lists precious metals as non-refundable
Costco's official return policy states a 100% satisfaction guarantee, then lists exceptions. One of them reads 'Precious metals are non-refundable.' The same document carves out diamonds of 1.00 ct or larger, which are returned against a Jewelry Credit Memo after a 48-hour gemologist inspection. Its jewelry FAQ also says jewelry bought on Costco.com can be returned for a refund including shipping. The captured gold ring is a 1.00 ct diamond ring, so which rule governs it is genuinely unclear from the published text.
 costco customerservice.costco.com Get Costco to confirm in writing (chat transcript or email with a document ID) that this specific item is returnable, and to which address, before paying. If they will not put it in writing, the checklist rule says do not buy.

How these were found

Eight automated passes run over the data on every build — see Method and reports/ in the repository. The three that produce this page are:

  1. Quote-substring pass: every recorded value must literally appear inside the quoted evidence captured from the page.
  2. Arithmetic pass: recomputes each derived number from its inputs and the recorded spot observation.
  3. Coverage pass: lists every field a row is missing, so blanks can never quietly become guesses.

A fourth, the money pass, scans every rendered page for $ figures and fails the build on any amount that cannot be traced to the data or to a formula printed on the page. That is the mechanical version of the no-hallucination rule.

Items marked “info” are recorded for completeness. “Warning” items need a written answer from the seller or a second source. “Critical” items are reserved for anything that could leave you unable to return the ring or that contradicts a seller's own published terms — there is one, and it is the reason Costco is not recommended for this purchase until it is answered in writing.
