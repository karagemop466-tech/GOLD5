Solid Gold vs. Plated / Filled / Overlay

A listing must explicitly say solid gold. Terms like plated, vermeil, gold-filled, gold overlay, or gold-tone mean the item is NOT solid gold throughout.

Term Meaning Is Solid Gold? FTC Requirement Source Verified Source Link
Solid gold The item is not hollow; it is gold alloy throughout. Also carries a karat mark (14K, 18K, etc.) Yes Must have karat mark (e.g., 14K, 18K) Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Gold plated Surface plating of gold alloy; plating wears away over time No Must state karat fineness; "gold plate" requires reasonable durability Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Gold filled / gold overlay / rolled gold plate (RGP) Layer of at least 10K gold mechanically applied to a base metal No Must be marked with karat quality and plating term (e.g., "14K gold overlay") Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Gold electroplate At least 10K gold, at least 0.175 microns thick, applied by electrolytic process No Minimum 10K fineness and 0.175 microns thickness Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Vermeil Sterling silver base coated or plated with gold No Must be sterling silver base with gold plating Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Gold flashed / gold washed Very thin electroplating; wears away faster than gold plate No No minimum thickness; ask seller to explain Verified FTC Consumer Guide
Gold-tone Color resembles gold but contains no gold No Not regulated as gold content Unverified FTC Consumer Guide

Red Flags — Avoid These Listings

  • Listing says "gold-tone," "gold color," or "gold finish" without saying "solid gold"
  • Listing says "gold plated," "gold plated over sterling," or "vermeil"
  • Listing says "gold filled" or "gold overlay"
  • No karat mark (14K, 18K, etc.) visible in product photos or description
  • Price is unusually cheap compared to gold weight — a major red flag

Source: FTC — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry
