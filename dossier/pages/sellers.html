⚠ NO HALLUCINATED PRICES — the governing rule of this project

  • Nothing here is estimated, remembered or inferred. Every price, karat claim, metal description and gram weight was read from an official first-party page (the retailer's own product page, the marketplace's own item page, or the benchmark administrator's own site) and stored with three things: the source URL, a UTC capture timestamp, and the literal verbatim excerpt that contains the number.
  • If a value could not be confirmed, the cell is left blank and the row is published on Flagged for review. A blank cell is a deliberate, honest result — never a gap filled by guesswork.
  • Automated anti-hallucination checks run at every build. Current run: 15/15 PASS, 0 FAIL. Check C3 re-parses every published price out of its own evidence excerpt and fails the build if the number is not literally there. Check C6 does the same for gram weights. See Verification.
  • Prices move. Retail jewellery prices and the gold price both change constantly, so every value is a timestamped observation, not a standing quote. Re-read the live page before paying — this dataset contains two documented cases where the same official page was indexed at two different prices (F-05, F-06).

How to check a seller's reputation (official method)

  • FTC — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry cited in research/*.md — not re-fetched this session (F-03) — do your homework on the seller; do not rely solely on claims made online or on social media.
  • The FTC's stated technique is to type the seller's name plus "complaint" or "review" into a search engine, and to ask people you know for recommendations. cited in research/*.md — not re-fetched this session (F-03)
  • Report problems to ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to your state attorney general via NAAG's consumer-complaint page. cited in research/*.md — not re-fetched this session (F-03)
  • The Jewelers Vigilance Committee operates a mediation programme for disputes with jewellers; Jewelers of America maintains a member directory. cited in research/*.md — not re-fetched this session (F-03)
  • Limitation (recorded in research/seller-reputation.md): no government-run seller-rating database for jewellers exists. BBB is a private organisation and the FTC's official guidance does not require it, so no BBB claim is made anywhere on this site.

Payment protection — and its limit

  • Credit cards are preferred because the Fair Credit Billing Act gives a dispute process: written dispute within 60 days of the first bill containing the error; the issuer must acknowledge within 30 days and resolve within two billing cycles, maximum 90 days; liability for unauthorized charges is capped at $50. FTC — Using Credit Cards and Disputing Charges cited in research/*.md — not re-fetched this session (F-03)
  • The limit that matters for this project: disputes about the quality of an item are not billing errors under the FCBA. A ring that assays below its stamped karat is a quality dispute. Chargeback rights are therefore weaker than for non-delivery, which is why the written return right and post-delivery independent testing carry the real protection.
  • Avoid irreversible payment methods. Nothing in this dataset records a payment, and no payment details are ever collected by this site.

Return-policy reality check

The FTC Cooling-Off Rule gives 3 days to cancel, but only for sales made at a home, workplace, dorm or the seller's temporary location (hotel, convention centre, fairground, restaurant), and it does not cover regular retail-store purchases or most online purchases. On cancellation the seller must refund all payments within 10 business days. FTC — Buyer's Remorse and 16 CFR Part 429 cited in research/*.md — not re-fetched this session (F-03)

Consequence: there is no general federal right to return an online jewellery purchase. The right comes from the seller's own written policy — which is why every return statement in the table above is quoted from the seller's page, with a link, instead of being assumed. Resized and custom rings are the usual exclusion; one eBay seller in this dataset advertises "Free Resizing" without stating whether a resized ring remains returnable.
