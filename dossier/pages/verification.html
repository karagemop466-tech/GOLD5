Verification methodology & hallucination checks

How every number on this site was obtained, and the automated passes that look for hallucinations. Current build: 15 of 15 checks PASS, 0 FAIL.

⚠ NO HALLUCINATED PRICES — the governing rule of this project

  • Nothing here is estimated, remembered or inferred. Every price, karat claim, metal description and gram weight was read from an official first-party page (the retailer's own product page, the marketplace's own item page, or the benchmark administrator's own site) and stored with three things: the source URL, a UTC capture timestamp, and the literal verbatim excerpt that contains the number.
  • If a value could not be confirmed, the cell is left blank and the row is published on Flagged for review. A blank cell is a deliberate, honest result — never a gap filled by guesswork.
  • Automated anti-hallucination checks run at every build. Current run: 15/15 PASS, 0 FAIL. Check C3 re-parses every published price out of its own evidence excerpt and fails the build if the number is not literally there. Check C6 does the same for gram weights. See Verification.
  • Prices move. Retail jewellery prices and the gold price both change constantly, so every value is a timestamped observation, not a standing quote. Re-read the live page before paying — this dataset contains two documented cases where the same official page was indexed at two different prices (F-05, F-06).

The rules every row had to satisfy

  • A price is recorded only if the literal price string was observed on an official first-party page (or in an index of it).
  • The literal observed string is stored in evidence.excerpt so any reviewer can diff the claim against its proof.
  • Every row carries evidence.source_url and an ISO-8601 UTC observed_at.
  • Missing values are null, rendered as a blank cell with a tooltip, and cross-listed on Flagged for review.
  • Karat, metal colour and gram weight are subject to the same rule as price: if the wording is not in the excerpt, the field is withdrawn (this is check C14/C15, and it withdrew four karat assertions).
  • Currency is recorded explicitly; rows observed only in a non-USD currency were not included.

Proof the passes bite: three real catches during this build

  1. C12 caught a wrong constant. The dataset first recorded 24K as 99.9% fineness. The check compares published fineness against karat ÷ 24 and failed. Investigation showed research/gold-purity.md records 99.99% with European mark 999, while 24/24 is nominally 100%. Both figures are now published with their sources instead of one being silently chosen.
  2. C14 caught an unsupported karat. Four Brilliant Earth rows asserted "14K white gold" that could only be inferred from the SKU suffix -14KW. The captured JSON-LD states name, SKU, brand, currency and price — not karat. The karat and metal fields were withdrawn to blank and flagged (F-14), rather than the check being loosened.
  3. C14 caught a missing excerpt. Row EBAY-236156740362 asserted 14K, but its stored excerpt described only the diamonds, the weight and the price. The listing's own title ("14k SOLID Gold Diamond Ring .50 TCW Sz 13 Modernist Design 5.6Gr.") was added verbatim to the excerpt, so the assertion now has evidence.

Known limitations, stated plainly

  • F-03 — the fetch proxy failed during most of this session. 3 of 58 rows were read from a live fetch before the outage; the remaining 55 rows are tier snippet. Their prices come from an index of the official page, each with the crawl date the index reported. That is honest but weaker evidence, and every such row says so on its face. Re-read the live page before paying.
  • Prices are observations, not offers. They change hourly; 16 observations taken on the same day differ by $71.50 per troy ounce.
  • A karat stamp, a seller's claim, a schema.org price field and even a gram weight are all claims. Only an independent assay (XRF or fire assay) after delivery settles purity — and the sources for XRF available to this project are vendor and industry publications, not government documents (F-02).
  • Nothing here is investment, legal or consumer-protection advice. It is a documented, auditable reading of public pages at a stated moment.

Recommendation

Look for hallucinations. After every review pass, re-run python3 scripts/build.py and read the failing checks first. Then spot-audit by hand: open three random rows, follow the source link, and confirm the price, karat and weight on the live page match the stored excerpt. If a number on this site cannot be matched to its excerpt, treat it as a hallucination, blank it, and raise a flag — that is the intended failure mode of this project, and blanking a value is always correct where guessing is not.
