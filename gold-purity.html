Karat gold — official definitions

Pure gold (24 karat) is soft, so it is mixed with other metals for hardness and durability. The karat mark tells you how much pure gold the piece contains: the total of pure gold and other metal adds up to 24 parts. Higher karat = more gold.

Karatage Karat mark European (millesimal) mark Percent pure gold Parts gold / parts other metal Source
10-karat 10K 417 41.7% 10 / 14 Jewelers of America — Quality Marks on Karat Gold Jewelry
14-karat 14K 585 58.3% 14 / 10 Jewelers of America · FTC
18-karat 18K 750 75.0% 18 / 6 Jewelers of America · FTC
24-karat 24K 999 99.99% 24 / 0 Jewelers of America
Key warning (FTC): a 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is actually that purity. Always look for the maker's/trademark mark alongside the karat mark, and verify by independent testing after delivery. See Hallmarks & Maker's Marks.

Legal basis

  • FTC, "Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry": 18K gold is 18 parts gold mixed with six parts of another metal; 14K gold is 14 parts gold mixed with ten parts of another metal. Source →
  • eCFR, 16 CFR § 23.3: it is unfair or deceptive to misrepresent the quantity or karat fineness of gold in a product. Source →
