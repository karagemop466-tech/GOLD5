How to pay — and why

Item Verified guidance Official source
Preferred payment Prefer a credit card for purchase protection. Checklist requirement (consumer guidance)
Avoid irreversible methods Avoid irreversible payment methods (e.g., wire transfers that cannot be disputed). Checklist requirement (consumer guidance)
Federal dispute law The Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) sets a dispute process for credit cards and other open-end credit accounts. FTC — Using Credit Cards and Disputing Charges
Dispute window Send a written dispute to the card issuer within 60 days of the first bill with the error. FTC
Issuer response Issuer must acknowledge in writing within 30 days and resolve within two billing cycles (max 90 days). FTC
Unauthorized charges Federal law limits your responsibility for unauthorized charges to $50 (many issuers offer zero-liability policies beyond the legal minimum). FTC
Covered billing errors Charges for items not accepted or not delivered as agreed, wrong amounts, unauthorized charges, etc. FTC — Billed for Things You Never Got
Not covered Disputes about the quality of the item are not billing errors under the FCBA. FTC
Credit vs debit Credit cards have stronger legal dispute protections than debit cards. FTC
Practical note: Credit-card protection is a reason to pay by card, but it does not replace checking the seller, the markings, the return policy, and independent verification after delivery.
