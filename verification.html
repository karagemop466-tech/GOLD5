After the ring arrives

Step What to do Source Source type
Independent jeweler Before wearing or resizing, take the ring to an independent jeweler and ask them to verify the gold purity and weight. Checklist requirement (consumer guidance) Guidance
Find a jeweler Use a jeweler directory to locate an independent professional (e.g., Jewelers of America "Find a Jeweler"). Jewelers of America Trade association
Dispute help If a dispute arises, the FTC points to ReportFraud.ftc.gov, the state attorney general, and the Jewelers Vigilance Committee (JVC) mediation program. FTC — Report Problems Government / NGO
XRF / professional purity test Ask for XRF testing or another professional gold-purity test; don't rely solely on the stamp. XRF is non-destructive and reads the metal's elemental composition. Thermo Fisher Scientific Industry / vendor
Flagged for review: No U.S. federal or state government source was found that mandates or describes consumer XRF testing. XRF content here comes from industry and vendor sources (instrument manufacturers and assay-office coverage). Independent-jeweler verification is sound consumer practice but is not a codified federal requirement. Both items are listed as unverified guidance on the Flagged for Review page — treat them as practical advice, not as regulatory requirements.
What XRF does and does not do (industry sources): XRF is non-destructive and identifies elemental composition, including gold content. It reads surface composition; plating beneath the surface may not be detected. A karat stamp alone is not proof of purity (see Hallmarks).
