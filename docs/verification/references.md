## 1. FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry
- URL: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-platinum-gold-and-silver-jewelry — **direct fetch**
- Verbatim: "A piece of jewelry marked 18K gold is 18 parts gold mixed with six parts of another metal. A piece marked 14K gold is 14 parts gold mixed with 10 parts of another metal."
- Verbatim: "Near the karat quality mark, look for the name or the U.S. registered trademark of the company that will stand behind the mark. The trademark may be a name, a symbol, or initials. If you're thinking about buying a piece of gold jewelry but don't see a trademark along with the karat mark, don't buy it."
- Verbatim: "The phrase 'solid gold' means the gold item isn't hollow. The item will also have a karat mark like 14K or 18K…"
- Verbatim: "'Gold filled,' 'gold overlay,' and 'rolled gold plate (RGP)' describe jewelry with a layer of at least 10 karat gold mechanically applied to a base metal… 'Gold electroplate' describes jewelry with a layer of at least 10 karat gold that is at least .175 microns thick… Vermeil is a special type of gold-plated product with a base of sterling silver coated or plated with gold."
- "Before You Buy" steps: ask people you know; search seller name + "complaint"/"review"; "Find the store's refund and return policies — and get them in writing."
- Reporting: ReportFraud.ftc.gov, state attorney general, JVC mediation (jvclegal.org).

## 2. 16 CFR Part 23 — Guides for the Jewelry, Precious Metals, and Pewter Industries
- URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-23 (official eCFR) — search snippet
- "§ 23.3 Misrepresentation as to gold content." — misrepresenting the presence/quantity/karat of gold (or of plating) is unfair or deceptive.
- FTC FRN PDF (official): https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/federal_register_notices/2015/12/151228jewelryguidesfrn.pdf — acceptable markings: "'14 Karat Gold,' '14 K. Gold,' or '14 Kt. Gold'"; karat designation may stand alone ("14 Karat," "14Kt.," "14 K.," "9 K.").
- Cornell LII mirror of §23.3: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/23.3 — search result (official law mirror).
