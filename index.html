⚠ NO HALLUCINATIONS — the rule that governs this entire project

Every price, weight, karat, hallmark claim and return policy on this site was read from the linked official source page on the check date shown. Nothing is estimated, averaged, filled in from a similar product, or copied from a search snippet without saying so.

  • Blank means unverified. If the seller's page does not state it, the cell stays empty and is published on Flagged for Review.
  • Every row has a source link to the exact page the value came from.
  • Derived numbers are calculated from verified inputs: fine gold = grams × karat fineness; value = fine gold × spot.
  • Priorities: official government/standards sources for rules; the seller's own page for its price and policy.
  • After every review pass run python3 scripts/verify.py — it re-checks sources, arithmetic, links and every dollar figure on the site.

How to read “price ÷ metal value”A ratio near 1 means you are paying mostly for gold. A high ratio is normal for a ring with diamonds and labour — it is not proof of a bad deal, and a low ratio is not automatically a bargain. The one signal to treat as a hard stop: a price below the metal value (ratio under 1.00). Only complete rings are comparable; setting prices exclude the center diamond. Full analysis →

How to use this in 15 minutes

Step 1 · 3 min
Read the checklist
The 12 checks, in order. If a seller cannot satisfy the weight, solid-gold, hallmark and return items, stop.
Step 2 · 5 min
Pick 2–3 listings
Filter by karat and seller, open each source page, and confirm the price and karat are still what the row says.
Step 3 · 4 min
Run the numbers
Ask for the gram weight, then use the calculator. A complete ring priced below its metal value is a stop sign.
Step 4 · 3 min
Ask, in writing
Weight, solid gold, hallmark photo, return window and resize exclusions. Keep the replies with your receipt.
