Facts are only as strong as their source.

The tracker currently includes a 14K yellow-gold setting listing and a complete 1 ct ring listing with a 14K gold band and platinum head. Neither is purchase-ready: their pages do not confirm the gold-alloy weight or physical hallmark/maker mark, and neither explicitly states solid gold in the reviewed listing. The Blue Nile price is setting-only; the Quince listing is a complete-ring price, with different material details.

When reviewing any price or purity claim, return to this no-hallucinations rule: link it, date it, state exactly what it covers, and leave it blank when it cannot be confirmed.

What “verified” means here

Seller-stated ≠ independently tested. A product page can support what that seller says about its own listing. Only an independent jeweler's professional test after delivery can check the specific ring's actual purity.

Current raw gold price is left empty. Do not compare a ring against an invented or undated spot price.

Your buying checklist

Do these checks before purchase

1

Confirm the listing

Require explicit solid gold, karat, hallmark plus maker mark, weight in grams, and a full itemized price. Keep the seller page, receipt, and claims.

2

Check return and payment terms

Confirm inspection and return rights, including resized/custom exclusions. Prefer a credit card; avoid irreversible payment. Do not proceed if independent verification or a return is blocked.

3

Test before wearing or resizing

Ask an independent jeweler for professional gold-purity testing—XRF or another suitable method—and a measured weight. Do not rely on a stamp alone.
