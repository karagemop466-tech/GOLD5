How the price-vs-gold test works

1. Get the ring's weight in grams (ask the seller — a legitimate seller can provide it).
2. Pure gold grams = weight × karat ÷ 24 (14K → ×0.5833, 18K → ×0.75).
3. Raw metal value = pure gold grams × spot $/g, re-checked the day you buy.
4. Compare: below metal value is a fraud signal (nobody sells solid gold below melt). A ring priced at a small multiple of melt can still be legitimate if the workmanship and stones justify it — but the lower the multiple, the harder you should look at the seller.

Two rules of thumb that are worth more than any single quote

A price below melt is not a bargain

Gold is a commodity with a public price. If a seller offers solid 14K gold for less than its scrap value, either the gold is not what it claims to be, or the “solid gold” description is wrong. Case study F1 in this dataset priced at 0.84× melt.

You are buying metal plus work, not a number on a stamp

Setting-only prices (Blue Nile, Brilliant Earth, Allurez) exclude the center stone, so their metal ratios look low. Complete-ring prices (Avianne, Jewelry Master, Etsy) include stones, so they look high. Compare like with like — F10.
