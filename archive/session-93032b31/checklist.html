The 12-point online gold-ring checklist

Each point is tied to an official source and to what this project actually observed across 24 verified listings. The checklist is the buyer's requirement; the right-hand column is the evidence.

The key, restated

A 14K or 18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is that purity. The safe pattern is: reputable seller with real contact details + karat and maker's mark + a published weight + a sensible price against the gold market + a return window that survives resizing + credit-card payment + independent XRF testing after delivery. If the seller won't let you verify it or return it — don't buy it.

1. Gold purity (karat)

Confirm 10K / 14K / 18K and what it means: 14K = 14/24 gold, 18K = 18/24.

What we found: Observed range across the 24 listings: 14K and 18K only. Karat is recorded per row with its source.

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

2. Solid gold

The listing must say solid gold — not plated, vermeil, gold-filled, overlay or gold-tone. FTC: 'solid gold' means not hollow.

What we found: 9 listings state 'solid' explicitly. The rest are silent — silence is not proof, so those cells are blank and flagged.

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

3. Hallmark + maker's mark

A karat stamp is not enough: FTC says look for the maker's name or trademark next to it, and if it is missing, don't buy.

What we found: No listing in this dataset shows a hallmark photo with a maker's mark. That is the single biggest gap — flagged on every row.

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

4. Weight in grams

A legitimate seller can provide the ring's weight in grams.

What we found: 8 listings publish a gram weight; 16 do not — left blank, flagged F3. Weights published by sellers are claims (F19).

Official source: NIST — Precious Metals Conversion Information prior capture

5. Seller reputation

Search the business name plus 'complaint' and 'review'; verify real contact details.

What we found: Phone/email captured where published. Blue Nile has a real but mixed record (F4). Etsy shops show shop ratings, not item ratings (F16).

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

6. Price vs. gold value

Compute pure gold grams x current spot; an unusually cheap price is a red flag.

What we found: Computed for every row with a weight. One case study priced below melt (F1). The rest sit above metal value, as expected.

Official source: Kitco — Live gold price and 'Gold Value by Purity' table re-verified

7. Return policy

Confirm you can return after inspecting it; check exclusions on resized/custom rings.

What we found: Blue Nile excludes resized/engraved/custom (F5). Etsy windows differ per listing: 7 / 14 / 21 / 30 days (F15).

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

8. Payment

Use a credit card for purchase protection; avoid irreversible methods.

What we found: All sellers checked accept card payment; several push financing (Affirm/Klarna) which changes your dispute route.

Official source: FTC — ReportFraud.ftc.gov prior capture

9. Documentation

Keep the listing, receipt, description and every purity claim the seller made.

What we found: This site keeps the source URL and check date for every row — save the same pages yourself at checkout.

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

10. Independent verification on arrival

Before wearing or resizing, have an independent jeweler verify purity and weight.

What we found: Plan this before you buy: a resize can void the return window (F5).

Official source: Jewelers Vigilance Committee — mediation services prior capture

11. XRF testing

Ask for XRF (or another professional assay); do not rely on the stamp.

What we found: No listing offers an XRF report. Budget for a test on arrival; the stamp alone is a claim (F11).

Official source: 16 CFR § 23.3 — Misrepresentation as to gold content (Cornell LII) prior capture

12. If the seller won't let you verify or return it — don't buy

Refusal to allow independent verification or a return is disqualifying.

What we found: Solid Gold Jewelers publishes no price and no return policy — treat as unusable until the seller answers in writing (flagged).

Official source: FTC Consumer Advice — Buying Platinum, Gold, and Silver Jewelry re-verified

How to verify a ring yourself, in order

  1. Before paying: save the listing page, the price, the karat claim, the solid-gold wording and the return policy (screenshot + URL).
  2. Ask the seller in writing for the weight in grams, the maker's mark, and confirmation that the item is solid gold, not plated or filled.
  3. Pay by credit card. Avoid bank transfer, gift cards, crypto, or anything off-platform.
  4. On arrival, before wearing or resizing: take it to an independent jeweler. Ask for the weight on a scale and an XRF test; get it in writing.
  5. Compare: measured weight vs. the seller's claim; measured purity vs. the stamp; measured metal value vs. what you paid.
  6. If anything disagrees: return it inside the window, in unworn condition, with the documentation you saved. Do not resize first — a resize usually voids the return right.
